Globally Optimal Training of Spiking Neural Networks via Parameter Reconstruction

With Xiaocong Yang and ChengXiang Zhai. Training an SNN normally depends on surrogate gradients, because the spike function is not differentiable, and the resulting approximation error accumulates across layers. We extend the convexification of parallel feedforward threshold networks to parallel recurrent threshold networks, which contain parallel SNNs as a structured special case, and use that to build a parameter reconstruction algorithm for training. It helps consistently across tasks, both on its own and alongside surrogate-gradient training, and the ablations show it scales with data and holds up across model configurations.

Bounding the gap between sampled and complete spike dictionaries

The previous paper proves global optimality for the training objective of parallel overparameterised SNNs, but the object that result depends on — the spike dictionary — is computationally intractable to enumerate. In practice we fall back on sampling heuristics, which carry no guarantee of stationarity and no bound on their distance from the optimum. This work aims to bound that gap theoretically: how far a sampled dictionary can sit from the complete one, and what that costs the optimality guarantee.

Practical direct training algorithms for globally optimal SNN training

Recent work establishes the stationarity of an optimal-transport-inspired training algorithm for non-differentiable objectives, which is exactly the setting a spike function puts you in. The aim here is to adapt that algorithm so it holds up on large parallel spiking networks, where the cost per step and the number of parameters both stop the original formulation from being run directly. The target is a method that keeps the theoretical footing while being cheap enough to train at a useful scale, and that can stand alongside surrogate gradients rather than only replacing them.

Pilanci Lab Optimal transport Ongoing

Generalisation guarantees for overparameterised parallel SNNs

Work on convex reformulation has largely gone into solving the training objective to optimality, which says nothing about which problems the method is suited to in the first place. This project asks what populations it can handle. The parameter we are working with for now is separability between classes, and the goal is a relation between that separability and how well the trained network generalises.

B-EDIS: measuring overthinking in reasoning chains

An early-stage framework that treats a language model's reasoning trace as a dynamical system and scores its instability across batched samples. The open problem is segmentation — deciding where one reasoning step ends — which I'm approaching with Self-Ask and Socratic scaffolds so the measure transfers across domains.

zScore: A Universal Decentralised Reputation System for the Blockchain Economy

With Ashutosh Sahoo, Akshay S. P., Gurukiran S., Parag Paul and Petrus C. Martens. The onchain economy is built on trustless peer-to-peer interaction, which leaves it without a way to quantify credibility and so without a way to price bad activity. zScore is a reputation primitive derived from a wallet's onchain behaviour using neural network models, combined with real-world credentials ported onchain through zkTLS. On retroactive data from lending protocols, a good zScore correlates strongly with healthy borrowing and repayment. The system flagged the February 2025 Aave liquidation event ahead of it, identifies risky wallets at a 90–95% rate, and pushes validation of its own outputs onchain, secured by slashing.
